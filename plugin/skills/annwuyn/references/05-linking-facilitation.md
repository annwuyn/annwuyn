# 5 — Linking facilitation: scope, distance, explicitness

Source: sessions S18–S24. Three levers that make an already-linear, already-progressive text
actually readable to a native academic reader.

---

## A. Linking scope

*What does this element attach to?* Scope errors are the commonest source of sentences that
"sound fine" and mean the wrong thing.

Phrase structure (from *Summary of tree basic structures*):

```
NP   = (D) (AdjP)+ N (PP)+
PP   = (AdvP) P NP (PP+)
AdvP = (AdvP) Adv
AdjP = (AdvP) Adj (PP)
VP   = (AdvP+) V (NP) (NP|PP) (AdvP+)      intransitive / transitive / ditransitive
CP   = NP VP        TP = T VP → CP = NP TP
T: tense (present/past/future) · aspect (perfect/progressive) · voice (passive) ·
   negativity · modality · non-finite marker -to · gerund marker -ing
Order inside T: modality/tense → negation → aspect → voice → verb
```

**Head identification: RIGHT then LEFT, NEAR to FAR.**
**Substitution must cover the head** — *the big yellow box of crayons* and *the small **one*** ✓;
*the small one **of pencils*** ✗.
**Binary principle** — every attachment must be a legal two-way join. In
*always in true love with his wife*: `his + wife` ✓ → `with + (his wife)` ✓ →
`love + (with his wife)` ✗ ⇒ so the PP attaches higher, not to *love*.
**Separation test** distinguishes complement from adjunct.

| | complement | adjunct |
|---|---|---|
| necessity | essential | optional |
| quantity | ≤ 1 | any number |
| linking scope | same as HEAD | higher |
| movement | fixed | more flexible |

Movement is allowed **within the same scope without meaning change**; across scopes it changes
meaning or breaks. *always kiss Kate quickly* ✓ / *always quickly kiss Kate* (different scope) /
*always kiss quickly Kate* ✗.

### Scope error patterns

- **Conditional scope leak.** *If advertisement did not exist, new concepts would never be
  brought to our mind. **Thus**, we would continue buying the same products.* — the second
  sentence has escaped the hypothetical. Fix: keep it inside
  (*…and subsequently we would continue…*), or set the scope up front
  (*Consider a hypothetical situation in which…* / *If this were the case, it would follow that…*).
- **Material trapped in the wrong branch.** *Older family members may experience the same
  problems (A), and they may succeed or fail (B). Therefore, they are in a better position to
  give advice (C).* — B is not a premise for C. Fix: *…(A). Therefore, **regardless of whether
  they succeeded or failed in solving such problems (B)**, they probably are in a better
  position…(C).*
- **Negation/coordination scope.** *His criticisms are not persuasive and unreasonable* — is
  *unreasonable* inside the negation? Factor out explicitly.
- **Infinitive scope.** *To better manage their time AND balance study and fun* (factored out,
  one *to*) vs *…AND **TO** balance…* (two separate purposes). Choose deliberately.
- **Adverb scope.** *how badly people treated him and stole money from him* → *people treated
  him **badly** and stole money from him* (badly should not scope over *stole*).
- **Comma and `that` define scope.**
  *She got hired by Sumitomo, one of the three biggest groups in Japan**,** after her graduation.*
  *The author wrote **that** the novel was a big success.*
  *You complained **that** in Vietnam you were overcharged* ≠ *You complained in Vietnam **that**
  you were overcharged.*
  *Researchers agreed THAT X BUT Y* ≠ *…THAT X BUT **THAT** Y* (parallel vs sub scope).
- **Word order = scope.** *God makes people crazy* ≠ *God makes crazy people*.
  *paint a door green* ≠ *paint a green door*. *contribute more substantially* ≠ *contribute
  substantially more*.
- **Avoid flat structures.** Coordinating everything at one level destroys the hierarchy that
  carries your argument. Sub scope vs parallel scope is a rhetorical choice.

## B. Linking distance

Minimise the distance between elements that must link. Use the **central position** for the
element that has to reach both ways.

- *The goal was to **successfully** build a pattern classifier to predict whether…*
  → *The goal was to build a pattern classifier to **successfully** predict whether…*
  (the adverb belongs next to what it modifies)
- *I told my parents about it in the very first stage, when I even did not have a clear idea of
  how to do, **in hope of getting their suggestions**.* — the purpose is stranded.
  → chain first: *I wanted to run a project → but did not yet know how → turn to my parents →
  for encouragement and help.*
- *many subjects, which I initially thought have little relevance to my major, **such as** higher
  mathematics…* → move the exemplification next to its head:
  *many subjects, **such as** advanced mathematics, quantum physics, and inorganic chemistry,
  which I initially thought…*
- *Thai Binh is considered one of the most prone regions to flood* → *one of the regions **most
  prone to flood***.
- *treatment of a common disease in the elders* → *treatment of a disease **common in the
  elders***.
- **VN vs EN ordering**: Vietnamese says *A khác cái gì so với B*; English says *A **so với B**
  khác cái gì*. → *parents, **in comparison to friends**, often influence young adults more and
  in different ways.*
- **Paraphrase to overcome distant linking.** When A and B are far apart, do not repeat A —
  restate it compactly at the point of use: *Even if **the so-called diet–aggressiveness
  relationship** holds true…*

Distance interacts with linearity and scope; fixing one usually improves the others.

## C. Linking explicitness

Three causes of over-implicit linking:

1. **the writer's failure to give full context**
2. **the writer's assumed ready access to underlying schemas**
3. **the writer's expectation that readers can interpret implicit links**

### Consequences
- lack of linearity and thematic disruption
- incorrect use of linking words (*Thanks to that,…* where nothing licenses it)
- argument flaws — arguments that "seem not tight enough"
- cross-cultural miscommunication

### Solutions
- employ more **explicit conjunctive links**
- **unpack** where necessary
- avoid over-reliance on **cultural schemas**
- perform **metaphor conversion**

→ In one word: **be writer-responsible**, not reader-responsible.

### Supplying background
- *When I watched Thai noodle advertisement in **VTV3***
  → *…on VTV3, **the most watched TV channel in Vietnam**…*
- *discharged waste water to the **Thi Vai River in Dong Nai***
  → *…**in Dong Nai Province of South Vietnam***
- *Hanoi has 111 lakes and **1165 ha** of water surface* → *1165 hectares **(equiv. to 2867
  acres)***
- a scandal reference that assumes the reader knows the story → name the referent and gloss it.

### Unpacking
- *In other words, thousand-year will be wasted to start again what people did before.*
  → *…a thousand years' worth of knowledge and experience would be wasted on **reinventing the
  wheel**.*
- Culture-specific metaphor + implied inter-sentential link = **multi-layer schema**:
  *Ai có công mài sắt sẽ có ngày nên kim. Đức kiên nhẫn … dẫn đến thành công.*
  Two options: use a **comparable metaphor in the target culture**, or **unpack**.
- Ambiguous implicit relation: *Both family and friends are very important **and** each has
  specific influences on our life* — cause-effect or contrast? Decide, then mark:
  *both are important **because/for** they both have great influence* vs
  *both are important **while/but** in different ways*.
- *Food plays such an important role in our life and now it is much easier to prepare.*
  → ***Since** food plays such an important role in our life, **it is satisfactory that** it is
  now much easier to prepare.*
- Implicit linking can produce an apparently *incorrect* linking word:
  *His sister … got a scholarship. **Thanks to that**, he always strives in his study…*
  → *His sister won a scholarship, making their parents proud. **Not to be outdone in that
  respect**, my boyfriend also strives hard.*

### Schema activation
A text whose schema is not activated is unreadable even when every sentence is clear (the
classic "the procedure is actually quite simple" paragraph). Before a difficult passage, name
the frame.
