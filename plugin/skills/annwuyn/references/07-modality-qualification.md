# 7 — Modality and Qualification

Source: sessions S24, S26–S30 (modality — an extended departure from Tyler's force-based account)
and S31–S32 (qualification).

Modal verbs are the main instrument of **claim qualification**, and they are both
**incorrectly used and underused** by Vietnamese writers.

---

## A. The force-based (cognitive) account

Function-based lists (*may/could = permission*, *should = advice*) are ambiguous and break down
completely in reasoning contexts. Model each modal instead as a **set of forces**:

- **source** — internal · external · mixed
- **strength** — depends on social and status relations; the past-tense form **weakens** the force
- **direction** — forward (thrust) vs backward (drag)

Three roles: **Author = force coordinator · Modal subject = force receiver · Force contributor**.
The force contributor plays a key role for internal forces, none for external, some for mixed.

Two parallel worlds, same force structure:

- **Social world** — obligation, permission, advice
- **Logical world** — prediction, inference. Internal vs external forces map to
  **subjectivity vs objectivity**; force strength maps to **certainty**.

## The modals

| Modal | Social | Logical | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WILL** | *You WILL submit your paper today* — very strong, internal | *That WILL be Tom* — near-certain, but **subjective** (opinion/belief based) | Rarely right for academic prediction |
| **GOING TO** | plan (external) vs WILL = willingness (internal) | evidence-based (external): *Our party is going to win. We already have most of the votes.* | |
| **MUST** | irresistible external force — irresistible *from the speaker's perspective* | *That MUST be Tom* — very strong, based on **objective evidence** | vs WILL: similar strength, different source; WILL still stronger but more subjective |
| **HAVE TO** | (AmE) internal + external combined; the external part is societal norms | | MUST = irresistible external force |
| **SHOULD** | external force = a **past pattern** [objective] → by subjectification, a **moral force** [subjective]; internal force signals conformity | *At 8 o'clock, that SHOULD be Tom* — past patterns / statistical data as external force | |
| **SHALL** | stronger external force, stronger authority and binding obligation | | Now mostly legal + fixed expressions; replaced by WILL |
| **WOULD** | | intuitions / personal experience / predisposition — **internal** forces | SHOULD & WOULD ≪ MUST & WILL in strength |
| **COULD** | *You COULD submit your paper today* — **opposite forces involved** | *That COULD be Tom (but it could also be someone else)* — weakened support; less strong than WOULD & SHOULD | |
| **CAN** | actual ability | **the only English modal with no logical extension** — *That CAN be Tom* ✗ (only *can't* survives, as physical impossibility) | CAN vs COULD = ability vs possibility |
| **MAY / MIGHT** | no actual force imposed, barriers removed → freedom to move → permission | *That MAY/MIGHT be Tom* — no evidence preventing the prediction, none compelling it → **weakest prediction** | |

**Two refinements from standard grammars** (`14-lecture-notes.md` §10). Quirk et al. (1985),
Biber et al. (1999) and Huddleston & Pullum (2002) class *That can’t be Tom* and *She can’t have
seen it* as epistemic: logical impossibility, the negative counterpart of MUST. In research prose
write *cannot* for a logical exclusion. Corpus studies report that deontic MUST has declined in
British and American English while HAVE TO and NEED TO rose (Leech et al., 2009).

**The SHOULD trap.** Vietnamese writers use SHOULD instead of MUST to sound less assertive and
more polite. Because SHOULD carries a moral dimension, the unintended effect is that they sound
like they are **lecturing**. Check every SHOULD in a proposal or recommendation.

**COULD vs WOULD in claims.**
*His claim … **WOULD** be either an exaggeration or simple ignorance* → ***COULD** be either*
(you are offering alternatives, not predicting).
In an LOR: *his qualities **COULD** make him a good researcher* → *his qualities **make** him a
good researcher* — COULD undercuts the recommendation.

**MAY/MIGHT** defend a viewpoint *or* concede to a counter-argument — check which you mean:
*This point **COULD** vs **MAY/MIGHT** be partly illustrated by the following example.*

## Modality + aspect

A modal carries no tense, so the **reference point = the assertion time**.

| Aspect | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ / simple | | *We COULD go to the movie.* |
| perfect | | *She COULD have stayed up late, but she went to bed early.* |
| progressive | | *You COULD be enjoying our newly offered dinner service at this very moment.* |
| perfect + progressive | | *I COULD have been relaxing at home if I didn't have to work today.* |

To indicate future sense: (1) use WILL and replace the modal with a non-modal structure that
also carries modality — *By next year all the money WILL probably have been spent*;
*70% of jet fuels … WILL have to be green by 2050*; or (2) move the reference point forward
explicitly — *This time next Friday you COULD be enjoying…*; *I SHOULD have finished the book
by Friday.*

**MODAL + have done**
- Logical, guessing about the past → **all** modals:
  *He MIGHT have missed the train* · *They COULD have just forgotten to call* ·
  *They SHOULD have finished by seven* · *That WOULD have been our neighbour's car* ·
  *She MUST have left already* · *The bus WILL have departed by now.*
- Logical, counterfactual: *They WOULD never have met if she hadn't gone to Emma's party.*
  Conditions may be only implied: *You COULD have hurt yourself!*
- Social, wishing it had been different → modals with an **internal** force: SHOULD, WOULD.
- Social, possible-in-the-past-but-no-longer → **COULD**: *She COULD have stayed up late, but…*

## Subjectification

Objective meaning becomes infused with the speaker's subjective perspective. It runs across the
grammar, not only modality:

- `while`, `meanwhile`: SEQUENCE [objective] → CONTRAST [subjective]
- present progressive: *We are talking to Susan on the phone* [ongoing, objective] →
  *We're meeting Susan at 3 p.m.* [future, subjective — the situation is conceptualised as
  already in progress]. It spreads GO → other motion verbs → non-motion verbs, and near future
  → distant future.
- SHOULD: past pattern → moral force
- MUST: strong internal desire → external force
- CAN: objectively possible → subjectively impossible
  (*He CANNOT make judgment about a whole country's education based solely on…* — actually he
  can and did, but he **SHOULD NOT**)
- COULD: internal desire/reservation → external forward/backward force

## B. Qualification

Unqualified = **absolute** in academic writing. Every unqualified claim is a claim of absolute
truth, and will be read as one.

### Techniques

**1. Knowledge-scope markers** (for gap identification)
*There are no other studies on X* → ***To the best of my knowledge | To my knowledge**, there
are no other studies on X* (*As far as I know* is [I]).
*The solar system's nearest planetary system is…* → *…nearest **known** planetary system…*

**2. Narrow down or broaden the claim's scope**
- *There are three differences between my parents' generation and mine* → ***at least three
  life-style | important** differences* (expansion)
- *advertising is good because it introduces new products* → *advertising is good **in that**
  [restrictive] it introduces new products*
- *This is the worst show ever* → *This is the worst show **I ever watched*** (contraction) or
  *This **could be deemed one of** the worst TV shows ever* (expansion)
- *Reading books is **the** good way* → *…**one of the better** ways*

**3. Re-define a concept to limit the argument scope**
- *…different opinions, **by which I mean** the view we hold about friendship, work, love…*
- *For the sake of this argument, I would define scandals as…*
- *Scandals herein, **for all intents and purposes** [F, LG], are defined as…*
- *Future developments are **beyond the scope of this essay**.*

**4. Put claims in perspective**
- *our defense budget is small* → *…is **relatively | comparatively** small* / *…is **fairly
  modest set against that of** some other countries, such as…*
- *his chosen topic is innovative and ambitious* → *…innovative and ambitious **in the context
  of Vietnam***

**5. Attribute rather than assert**
- *South Korea, an Asian miracle, is a typical example* → *South Korea, **considered** an Asian
  miracle **by** [named source], **could be cited as a fairly** typical example*
- *this is not Vietnam* → *this is not **the Vietnam I know | the Vietnam in my book | the
  Vietnam as I see it***

**6. Modal qualification** — the main instrument.
*Getting a job while attending university **distracts** students from their study, which
**gives** them necessary specialized knowledge* → *…**may/could** distract…, which
**supposedly** gives…*

**7. Conditional qualification** — qualify each link in the chain, not just the conclusion.
*the public transport system improves → **[probably]** people feel safer → **[probably]** use it
more regularly*
→ *When such a system becomes more widely available and reliable, people **would feel more
inclined** to use their service **SHOULD** they find it safer, cheaper and more convenient than
their private counterparts.*

### ALWAYS qualify when
- making an **inference**: *As inferred from the article, you **conclude** that…* → *you
  **apparently assume** that…*
- making a **sweeping** claim: *the first thing **every tourist shares** about a new place* →
  *…**would share**…*

### Over-qualification is also an error
- *Most of our discussions are **usually** productive.* — economy of language
- *Every family **in general** raises at least one kind of domesticated animals.* — logic
- *Mandatory retirement at 65 is **generally** lawful **in some countries**.* — linking scope
- *This example is not true **to some extent**.*
- *You **could** forget to mention that China attempted to invade Vietnam for centuries* →
  *You **failed** to mention…* (it is a fact; no qualification)
- *These street vendors surely do not represent most of Vietnamese, **who** are open-minded* →
  *…**most of whom** are open-minded and hospitable* (the qualifier must reach the right scope)

Hedging and uncertainty markers in English **open up argument space for readers to
participate**. To Vietnamese speakers they may seem unnecessarily indirect. They are not
optional politeness — they are how the argument is made.
