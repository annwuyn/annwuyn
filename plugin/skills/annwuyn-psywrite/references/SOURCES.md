# Sources and reading list

## Core book

Beins, B. C., & Beins, A. M. (2021). *Effective writing in psychology: Papers, posters, and
presentations* (3rd ed.). Wiley.

**In this library (photographed pp. 196–300):** ch. 15 “Final Touches: The Abstract and
Formatting Details” (pp. 196–219, with gaps); Part III “Communicating Beyond the Research Paper”:
ch. 16 posters, ch. 17 oral presentations, ch. 18 presenting work on the internet (pp. 247–254,
a web-publishing walkthrough; not summarised because the tools have changed), ch. 19 ethics
committees; Appendix A (annotated manuscript with common errors, pp. 265–272); Appendix B
(corrected manuscript, pp. 273–); the reference list and author index.

**Not yet in this library:** pp. 1–195, including the book’s chapters on the Introduction,
Method, Results and Discussion, style and grammar, and references. Pages 220–223, 232–237,
240–241 and 264 are also missing. When they are photographed, add a section per chapter to the
matching reference file with page numbers.

## Introductions: genre research

- Swales, J. M. (1990). *Genre analysis: English in academic and research settings*. Cambridge
  University Press. — the CARS model.
- Swales, J. M. (2004). *Research genres: Explorations and applications*. Cambridge University
  Press. — revised CARS model.
- Feak, C. B., & Swales, J. M. (2011). *Creating contexts: Writing introductions across genres*.
  University of Michigan Press.
- Swales, J. M., & Feak, C. B. (2012). *Academic writing for graduate students* (3rd ed.).
  University of Michigan Press.
- Nguyen, T. T. L., & Pramoolsook, I. (2014). Rhetorical structure of introduction chapters
  written by novice Vietnamese TESOL postgraduates. *3L: The Southeast Asian Journal of English
  Language Studies, 20*(1), 61–74.

## Introductions: writing guides

- Bem, D. J. (2004). Writing the empirical journal article. In J. M. Darley, M. P. Zanna, &
  H. L. Roediger III (Eds.), *The compleat academic* (2nd ed., pp. 185–219). American
  Psychological Association. — the hourglass; open with people and their behaviour.
- Kendall, P. C., Silk, J. S., & Chu, B. C. (2000). Introducing your research report: Writing
  the introduction. In R. J. Sternberg (Ed.), *Guide to publishing in psychology journals*
  (pp. 41–57). Cambridge University Press. (A second edition of the volume appeared in 2018.)
- Grant, A. M., & Pollock, T. G. (2011). Publishing in *AMJ*—Part 3: Setting the hook. *Academy
  of Management Journal, 54*(5), 873–879. https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2011.4000 — quote and
  trend hooks, from award-winning articles.
- Lingard, L. (2015). Joining a conversation: The problem/gap/hook heuristic. *Perspectives on
  Medical Education, 4*(5), 252–253. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-015-0211-y (open access)
- Heard, S. B. (2016). *The scientist’s guide to writing*. Princeton University Press. — ch. 8
  (IMRaD and the hourglass) and ch. 10 (the Introduction). A copy is in the user’s Drive,
  `08 Personal/Linh tinh/`.
- Schimel, J. (2012). *Writing science: How to write papers that get cited and proposals that
  get funded*. Oxford University Press. — story structures for openings.
- Silvia, P. J. (2015). *Write it up: Practical strategies for writing and publishing journal
  articles*. American Psychological Association.
- Gernsbacher, M. A. (2018). Writing empirical articles: Transparency, reproducibility, clarity,
  and memorability. *Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science, 1*(3), 403–414.
  https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245918754485

## APA style and reporting standards

- Appelbaum, M., Cooper, H., Kline, R. B., Mayo-Wilson, E., Nezu, A. M., & Rao, S. M. (2018).

  Psychologist, 73*(1), 3–25. https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000191
- Levitt, H. M., Bamberg, M., Creswell, J. W., Frost, D. M., Josselson, R., & Suárez-Orozco, C.
  (2018). Journal article reporting standards for qualitative primary, qualitative
  meta-analytic, and mixed methods research in psychology. *American Psychologist, 73*(1),
  26–46. https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000151
