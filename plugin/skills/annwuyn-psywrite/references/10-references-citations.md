# 10 — Citations in the text and the reference list

Source: Beins & Beins (2021), ch. 14, pp. 177–193, checked against the APA *Publication Manual*
(7th ed.), ch. 8–10. For checking that each reference exists and matches its source, use
`/ars-citation-check` (academic-research-skills).

## Why and what to cite (pp. 177–178)

- References document the path of ideas that led to your question and conclusions.
- Choose the sources that develop each point; citing every source you met makes the text
  redundant.
- The reference list contains exactly the works cited in the text, and every work cited appears
  in it. Psychology uses a reference list; a bibliography of uncited works is a different genre.
- References start on a new page under **References** (centred, bold), double-spaced, no blank
  line after the heading, hanging indent (first line flush left, later lines indented 0.5 in.).
  Set the hanging indent with the word processor’s paragraph settings, never with manual line
  breaks (pp. 182–183).

## In-text citations (pp. 178–181)

- Surname(s) and year; initials only to separate two first authors with the same surname.
  Affiliations are never cited.
- **Narrative:** Kubey and Baker (2000) noted … **Parenthetical:** … (Baker, 2004).
- “and” in running text; “&” inside parentheses.
- **One or two authors:** name both every time.
- **Three or more authors:** first author + “et al.” from the first citation (Stephenson et al.,
  2006).
- **Group authors:** spell out the name; define an abbreviation at first use if you will reuse it:
  (American Psychological Association [APA], 2020), then (APA, 2020).
- **Year within a paragraph:** APA 7 drops the year from repeated narrative citations in the same
  paragraph; keep it in parenthetical citations.
- **Several works in one parenthesis:** alphabetical by first author, separated by semicolons;
  same author’s works by year, separated by commas; same author and year with a, b, c
  (Davis, 2004a, 2004b).
- **Personal communications** (emails, conversations, unpublished notes): in the text only, with
  initials, surname and exact date (J. Carlson, personal communication, March 3, 2020); never in
  the reference list (pp. 180–181).
- **Secondary sources:** cite what you read: Burnham (as cited in Rosnow & Rosenthal, 1993);
  only Rosnow and Rosenthal go in the list. Use sparingly; read the original when you can.
- Quotations need a page number (p. 12) or other locator (para. 4).

## Order of the reference list (pp. 181–182)

- Alphabetical by first author’s surname, letter by letter; “nothing precedes something” (Brown, J.
  before Browning, A.).
- Mac, Mc and M’ alphabetised as spelled.
- Same first author: single-author works first, by year; then multi-author works by the second
  author’s surname.
- Same authors, same year: order by title, add a, b, c to the year.
- Same surname, different authors: order by initials.
- Group author: alphabetise by the first significant word (ignore “The”, “A”).
- Never reorder the authors within a reference.

## Reference formats (pp. 183–193)

**Authors:** up to 20 authors listed in full. With 21 or more: the first 19, an ellipsis, then the
final author (p. 180).

**Journal article**

Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article in sentence case. *Journal Name in Title
Case, Volume*(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxxx

**Book**

Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of book in sentence case* (2nd ed.). Publisher. https://doi.org/xxxx

**Chapter in an edited book**

Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), *Title of book* (pp. xx–xx).
Publisher.

**Conference poster or paper**

Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day–Day). *Title* [Poster presentation]. Conference Name, City, Country.

**Web page, news, blog, encyclopedia entry, podcast, online book:** follow the templates the
chapter lists (pp. 188–193) or the *Publication Manual*, ch. 10. Retrieval dates only for pages
designed to change.

**Details that trip writers up**

- DOI as a full URL (https://doi.org/…), no “doi:” label, no full stop after it.
- Article titles in sentence case and roman; journal names in title case and italics, with the
  volume number italicised and the issue in roman parentheses.
- Book titles in sentence case and italics; no publisher location in APA 7.
- Retrieve the DOI from Crossref when the PDF lacks it.

## Tools

The book shows how to set hanging indents in Word (pp. 182–183). A reference manager (Zotero
with the APA 7 style) saves time; check its output against these rules, since imported metadata
is often wrong (capitalisation, missing issue numbers, DOI format).

## Checklist

1. Every in-text citation has a reference, and the reverse.
2. Spelling of names and years match between text and list.
3. “&” in parentheses and in the list; “and” in running text.
4. “et al.” from the first citation for three or more authors.
5. Personal communications only in the text.
6. Sentence case for article and book titles; title case and italics for journals.
7. DOI as https://doi.org/… for every work that has one.
8. Alphabetical order and hanging indent.
