# 02 — Abstract and APA manuscript format

Source: Beins & Beins (2021), ch. 15, pp. 196–219, checked against the APA *Publication
Manual* (7th ed., 2020). Page numbers refer to the book.

---

## The abstract (pp. 196–197)

- **Length:** about 250 words at most. Journals set their own limit; follow it.
- **Content:** the research question; participants and their relevant characteristics (age,
  gender, race/ethnicity, number; species if non-human); the method (experimental,
  correlational, factor-analytic…) with apparatus and materials; the results, with levels of
  statistical significance; conclusions and implications.
- **Self-contained:** a reader should understand it without the paper. Define every
  abbreviation. Standard units (minutes, metres) need no definition.
- **Tense:** present tense for conclusions (“The research reveals that…”); past tense for what
  was done and found (“Participants recalled…”).
- **Voice:** active verbs.
- **Format:** own page (page 2), the word **Abstract** centred and bold above it, one block
  paragraph with no first-line indent, numerals for all numbers except at the start of a
  sentence. Keywords below, indented, with the label *Keywords:* in italics. Student papers may
  skip keywords; the instructor decides (Figure 15.1).

## Title page (pp. 198–199)

- Title centred and bold, upper half of the page. No empty phrases such as “A study of”; the
  title should name the content.
- Authors and affiliations below. Co-authors at one institution share a line.
- Author note (professional papers): affiliation, changes of affiliation, acknowledgments and
  funding, contact details. Student papers usually give course, instructor and date instead.
- Running head (professional papers): a short title in capitals, at most 50 characters with
  spaces, flush left in the header; page number flush right on every page. Insert both with the
  word processor’s header function, never by typing them on each page. Student papers in APA 7
  carry a page number only, unless the instructor asks for a running head.
- Double-space everything; one-inch (2.54 cm) margins.

## Section order and headings (pp. 201–204)

**Single-study paper (Box 15.1):** Title (starts the Introduction) → **Method** →
*Participants*, *Materials/Apparatus*, *Procedure* (Level 2) → **Results** → **Discussion** →
**References** → tables → figures → appendices → footnotes.

**Multi-study paper (Box 15.2):** Abstract → Introduction → **Experiment 1** (Level 1) with
Method, Results, Discussion at Level 2 and their subsections at Level 3 → Experiment 2 →
**General Discussion** → References.

**New page for:** title page, abstract, references, appendices, author note, footnotes, figure
captions, and each table or figure placed after the references. Method, Results and Discussion
follow on without a page break.

**Heading levels (Table 15.2):**

| Level | Format |
|---|---|
| 1 | Centred, Bold, Title Case |
| 2 | Flush Left, Bold, Title Case |
| 3 | *Flush Left, Bold Italic, Title Case* |
| 4 | Indented, Bold, Title Case, Ending With a Period. Text continues on the same line. |
| 5 | *Indented, Bold Italic, Title Case, Ending With a Period.* Text continues on the same line. |

Most single-study papers need only Levels 1 and 2. Title case: capitalise words of four or more
letters, plus all nouns, verbs, pronouns, adjectives and adverbs.

## Appendices, footnotes, notes (pp. 198–200)

- Appendix: material useful to readers and not needed to follow the study (word lists, stimuli,
  a questionnaire you wrote). Cite a published questionnaire and leave it out of the appendix. Label
  Appendix A, B…; plural is “appendices”.
- Footnotes are rare in APA style because they pull the reader away from the argument. If an idea
  matters, put it in the text. Footnotes are fine for table notes (probability values) and for
  copyright permission for a reproduced table or figure.
- Footnote numbers: superscript Arabic numerals placed after punctuation, except before a dash.
  Refer back as “(see Footnote 1)”.

## Mechanics tables in the book

The book gives rule tables with examples for: capitalisation (Table 15.3), italics (15.4),
abbreviations (15.5), series within a sentence (15.6), quotations (15.7, Figures 15.5–15.6) and
punctuation (15.8). Short rules worth remembering:

- Capitalise “Experiment 2”, “Table 3”, “Chapter 7” (noun + number); do not capitalise
  condition or variable names in running text.
- Capitalise a department name when it is the proper name; keep job titles lower case.
- Italicise the anchors of a rating scale, statistical symbols (*F*, *p*, *t*, *M*, *SD*), and
  book and journal titles. Do not use italics for emphasis; build the emphasis into the syntax.
- Series: (a), (b), (c) within a sentence; numbered lists for separate sentences or steps.
- Quotations under 40 words go in double quotation marks in the text, with page number. Forty
  words or more go in an indented block with no quotation marks.
- Write out numbers below 10, and any number that starts a sentence.
- Use “&” inside parentheses and in the reference list; use “and” in running text.
- “Data” is plural: “the data show”.
